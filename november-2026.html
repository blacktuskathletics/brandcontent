Carousel 1 · What nobody tells you about an Edmonton winter (Acquisition) · 6 slides

Cover: WHAT NOBODY TELLS YOU ABOUT AN EDMONTON WINTER. "Four things we have learned from ten winters on this floor."

One, the cold is not the problem. The doorway is. Inside it is sixteen degrees and nobody has ever regretted being here. The whole fight happens in your kitchen at 5:40.

Two, you need a day, not a plan. Winter does not reward ambition. It rewards one protected day you actually keep, every week, boring and unglamorous, until March.

Three, warm up longer than you want to. Your tissue is genuinely colder in November. Ten extra minutes is the cheapest injury insurance you will ever buy.

Four, the room is the reason. People who train alone in winter stop in January. People who get noticed when they are missing do not. That is not a sales line, it is just how people work.

Email 2 · Tue Nov 10 · Retention · Subject: "Winter training is a skill, not a mood."

The clocks went back last week and the city got about four hours shorter. If training has started to feel harder since then, nothing is wrong with you. Something genuinely changed.

Almost nobody stops training in winter because they decided to. They stop because the plan quietly depended on feeling like it, and from roughly the first week of November onward, nobody feels like it. So the fix is not more willpower. It is making the plan depend on something else.

One, the fight happens in your kitchen, not at the gym. Once you are through the door it is sixteen degrees in here and you have never once regretted being here. So win the doorway: bag packed the night before, clothes out, no negotiation at 5:40 in the morning.

Two, pick one day and protect it like a dentist appointment. Not four. One. Tell us which one it is and we will notice when you are not in it.

Three, warm up longer than you want to. Your tissue is genuinely colder in November, and ten extra minutes is the cheapest injury insurance you will ever buy. Ask a coach on your way in and we will walk you through it.

Four, if your schedule is in pieces this month, Personal Training exists for exactly that. One hour, built around the week you actually have, not the one you wish you had.

Email 4 · Wed Nov 25 · Retention · Subject: "The most efficient hour of your winter."

A thing we hear a lot in late November: "I do not have time right now." Almost always true, and almost always the reason people should be talking to us rather than waiting.

Personal Training is not the premium version of training here. It is the efficient one. One hour, built around your week, your body, your knee, your goal, and whatever you are genuinely able to give at this point in the year. Nothing is wasted figuring out what to do, because somebody already did that before you arrived.

It tends to be the right call when any of this is true. Your schedule changes week to week and classes never quite line up. Something hurts and you have been working around it for months. You have a specific goal, a race, a surgery to come back from, a sport your kid plays. Or you train already and suspect you are leaving a lot on the table, which you probably are.

It is also the most reliable way to keep training through a winter. One booked hour with a person expecting you is harder to skip than a class you could always catch tomorrow.

Original blog posts written this month

Blog A · "The Quiet Build" (mindset and retention)

November is nobody's favourite month. There is no race to point at, no holiday worth planning around, nothing to post. The clocks go back in the first week and the city loses its evenings in a single night. We think it is the most important month of the year, and we are aware of how that sounds.

Every gym in this city is full in January and thinner in November, and it is more or less the same people. What separates them is not fitness or discipline or wanting it enough. It is that a decision made in good conditions and a decision made in bad ones are different objects. One of them has been tested.

Think about what it takes to come in on a Tuesday in late November. Dark when you leave, dark when you get back, nothing pulling you here, and nothing bad happens if you stay home. The only thing getting you through the door is that you decided earlier, when you were warm and clear-headed, that this is a thing you do. That is a quiet build. Nobody sees it, and it will not look like anything for months. Then in April a hill you used to dread stops registering, and people call that progress as though it arrived in April. It did not. It was built in November by somebody who was not enjoying themselves.

So make the decision somewhere else. Pick the real day you have, not the one you wish you had. Pack the bag the night before, because the fight is in your kitchen at twenty to six. Let it be boring. And train somewhere people notice, because a room full of strangers is a place you can leave quietly and a room full of people who know you is a place you come back to.

Blog B · "Start In The Worst Month" (acquisition)

Every year, from about the middle of November, people tell us they are going to start in January. They mean it, and a good number of them do turn up. But we would like to make the case, honestly and without a discount attached, that January is the worse month to begin.

January is crowded. Every coach is split across more new people than usual, and the attention any one person gets is a fraction of what it is in the third week of November. If you are brand new, that matters more than almost anything else. And if you start now you arrive in January with eight weeks behind you, which is a completely different January than the one you are imagining.

Then the real argument. A resolution made on the first of January is made in ideal conditions: clean year, holidays over, everyone doing it. None of those conditions last, and by the second week of February the decision has to stand on its own for the first time. For a lot of people it does not, and that is not a character flaw. The decision was simply never tested. A decision made in late November already has been.

We are not going to dress it up. November is not a nice month to start in. But nothing about January is special either. It is a date. The only month that matters is the one where you actually walk in.
