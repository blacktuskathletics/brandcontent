Subject line: The snow landed on a long weekend. You came anyway.
Preview text: Ten years, a city's worth of burpees, four centimetres of October snow, and what November is about to ask of all of us.

Hey team,

October did not ease into anything. Edmonton's first snow of the season landed on Saturday October 10, right in the middle of the Thanksgiving long weekend: about four centimetres of wet snow, a high of one degree dropping to minus one overnight, and north winds gusting to forty. That was nearly a month earlier than last year's first snow. Then by Friday the sixteenth it was sixteen degrees again, because this is Edmonton and that is what it does. In between all of that, this gym turned ten and a lot of you did a frankly unreasonable number of burpees for somebody else's benefit. Here is a look back at October, and what November is going to ask of us.

A note on what November actually is

The quiet build.

November is nobody's favourite month. There is no race to point at, nothing to post, and in the first week the clocks go back and take the evenings with them. By the end of the month it is properly dark at half past four. Even people who love winter do not love this part of it.

We think it is the most important month of the year, and we know how that sounds. Every gym in this city is full in January and thinner in November, and it is largely the same people. What separates them is that a decision made in good conditions and a decision made in bad ones are different objects. Only one of them has been tested.

Nothing about this month will feel like progress. Do it anyway. The people who look strong in May are, without exception, the ones who were in here in November, not enjoying it, getting it done.

Coming up: the next few weeks

The clocks go back, the light goes with them, and this is the month that quietly decides your spring.

Sun Nov 1The clocks go back

Daylight saving ends overnight, so it is dark by five from here on and by half past four before the month is out. Here is the only plan worth having for it: pick one day you can genuinely protect, put it in your phone like a dentist appointment, and tell us which one it is so we notice when you are not in it. One booked day beats four imagined ones, every winter, without exception.
